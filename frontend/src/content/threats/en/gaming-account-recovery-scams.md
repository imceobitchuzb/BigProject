When a gamer loses access to an account containing years of achievements and rare cosmetics, panic often sets in. Fraudsters actively monitor community forums, subreddits, and social media replies to prey on this distress.

## 1. The "I Accidentally Reported Your Account" Trap

A classic social engineering script unfolds like this:
1. You receive a frantic message from an acquaintance or stranger: *"I'm so sorry! Someone with a name similar to yours scammed me, and I accidentally reported your Steam/Epic profile. A Valve employee said your account will be permanently banned within 2 hours unless you contact them on Discord!"*
2. They give you a Discord handle belonging to a fake "Valve Community Manager" or "Riot Support Agent."
3. The fake employee shows forged digital certificates or database screenshots.
4. They demand that you provide purchase receipts, temporary SMS codes, or transfer your valuable inventory items to a "quarantine holding account" for verification.
5. In reality: **Official platform staff never mediate bans, investigate reports, or offer support through third-party Discord chats.**

## 2. Pseudosupport "Recovery Hackers"

Under Reddit posts or tweets expressing frustration about a hijacked account, automated bots reply:
*"I lost my account too, but @cyber_recovery_pro on Instagram helped me retrieve it in 15 minutes! Contact him!"*
- These actors are 100% scammers.
- No outside individual has database write access to Valve, PlayStation Network, Xbox Live, or Riot Games servers.
- They will ask for an upfront "service fee" (\$50–\$200) paid via crypto or gift cards, take the money, and demand more funds under the pretext of "decryption keys."
- **Only the official customer support team of the game publisher has the authority and technical capability to restore account ownership.**
